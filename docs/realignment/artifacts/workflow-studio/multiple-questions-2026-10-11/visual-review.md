# Top-level and bounded-map questions: visual review

Date: 2026-10-11. Surface: owner Studio workflow card and paired-phone **Needs you** projection. This is a human review of opened Chromium captures, not an accessibility certification.

The journey used a real local SQLite store, the workflow engine and supervisor, typed HTTP API, and Chromium 153.0.8010.12 / Playwright 1.63. It made no model-provider calls; the browser used a local test assistant and simulated phone credentials. Captures cover 1440×960, 768×1024, 360×800 and 320×800 CSS pixels. The browser measured 0px horizontal overflow at every width and no page errors. Playwright role queries against Chromium's computed accessibility semantics verified that both question forms have distinct names containing their durable IDs and full prompts, and each answer textbox name repeats its question context and “Your answer” while exposing the authority-boundary help as its description. This verifies browser accessibility semantics; it does not verify behavior in an operating-system screen reader.

The browser journey discovers two questions from separate items in a bounded map configured with concurrency two. The engine regression additionally exercises two independent asks within one item and confirms a dependent ask remains held until its prerequisite answer arrives. Each question is paired with a durable ask-node record; both remain `Waiting` until all answers arrive, and a partial answer is labelled “Answer received,” not completed work. The waiting card makes the total explicit with “Needs you 2 pending questions.” After one answer, both the durable reason and the card show one remaining answer; when all answers arrive, the reason changes to continuation pending. The card keeps its established bounded internal scroll, so the first discovered item appears in the initial viewport; separate opened captures position the discovered-instance list near the top of the card at all four sizes. The long ID wraps at 320px without clipping. The second question remains readable and its answer and action are inside the same card. On phone widths the submit action is at least 44px high. After the first answer is submitted, the browser confirms focus moves to the remaining answer field and the partial-discovered capture shows one answered instance and one waiting instance. The completed capture shows four durable discovered instances (the map, two ask items and transform) with terminal “Completed” status, distinct from the immutable planned outline.

The compact layout inherits an internal scroll that means both full forms are not simultaneously visible at once. The count and reachable second card make the pending work discoverable, though simultaneous full visibility could improve scanning on larger screens. No design-system colors, typography or card patterns were changed beyond the pending-count and mobile-target styles.

Scores follow the project's visual review weighting (aesthetic 45%, functionality 40%, regression health 15%). Human judgments are marked as such.
