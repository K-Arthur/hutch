# Failures and complaints: what went wrong elsewhere, and what it means for Hutch

Mined 2026-11-06. Sources are in `01-SOURCES.md` section D (ids `D1` to `D21`); pass 1's tracker
mining is in `docs/security/vault-and-authentication/research.md` section 4 and is not repeated.
Requirement ids (`VS-R#`) are defined in `03-LESSONS-AND-DECISIONS.md`.

Reading rules:

- **Classification** is one of *fixable in Hutch* (Hutch controls the cause), *mitigable* (Hutch can
  reduce the harm but not remove the cause), or *vendor-side* (the cause is in a browser, OS,
  vendor service or model provider).
- **Hutch exposure** says what was checked. "Verified in code" means a line was read this session.
  "Not tested" means exactly that: nothing here claims Hutch is safe or unsafe on a point that was
  not run.
- These are reports from issue trackers, advisories and write-ups. Where a summary did not say the
  cause was established, the table says so. Nothing here is a claim about those products' current
  state.

## What the pattern says

1. **The common root is the environment, not the cryptography.** FC-1, FC-4, FC-5 and FC-6 are all
   "the secret was somewhere the agent could already read". The vault only helps once the old places
   are emptied, and `engine.py:409` shows the worker environment is the largest remaining one.
2. **Defenses that depend on a header or a timer are the ones that were bypassed**: forwarded-for
   (FC-8), lockout (FC-15), auto-lock (FC-14), clipboard clear (FC-11).
3. **Operational failures drive abandonment more than attacks do**: lost passphrase (FC-10), restore
   without key (FC-13), hangs (FC-7), re-prompt fatigue (FC-14). A feature that fails safe but silent
   will be turned off.
4. **Recovery is a hard design problem, not a footnote** (FC-9, FC-10, FC-13, FC-17).

## Not found, and gaps

- No vault, keychain or agent product was found whose mediated egress was shown to protect nothing;
  OpenClaw documents its own bypasses (A2) and that candor is the useful lesson. The planned search
  for "secret manager whose real egress was unmediated" returned no concrete case.
- Bitwarden or 1Password session-token leakage through the process list: no advisory found (C2). The
  environment-variable and argv exposure is documented by the vendors' own guidance, not by an incident.
- Reddit and Hacker News threads were not searched directly (see `01-SOURCES.md` section D).
- Screenshots, crash reports, shell history and password-manager autofill fighting the unlock field
  returned nothing citable this pass. They stay as test ideas (VS-R13, VS-R29), not as findings.
